# ADR-001: Serve zone pages from MongoDB, keep trips and corrections in a Delta table on HDFS

Status: proposed
Date:   2026-11-20

Every number below was measured on my laptop (WSL Ubuntu 24.04, 16 cores, 15 GiB, Spark `local[4]`, MongoDB 8.0.38, Hadoop 3.4.3) and is cited by manual section and by the log in `runs/` that holds it. A sentence that is not backed by a measurement says so.

## Context
What is stored: (a) zone figures for a web page (Q1: everything about one zone), (b) the trips analysts summarise (Q2: average fare per pickup hour over a month), (c) late corrections that arrive as a stream, as in Lab 3.

- Access pattern. Q1 reads 1 record out of 258 by key, once per page view. Q2 scans 2,724,143 January trips and reads 2 of 22 columns, a few times a day (manual Table 4; `sec9_compare.log`).
- Scale. The zone summary is 7.4 K on HDFS; one month of trips is 57.5 M as Parquet and 356.5 M as CSV (`sec2_inputs.log`, `sec4_write.log`). The same month takes 216.5 MB on disk and 1,319.8 MB unpacked in MongoDB (`sec9_trips.log`).
- Consistency. While a nightly job rewrote a plain Parquet directory, 0 of 149 reads saw a whole table (146 saw 0 rows, 3 failed); with Delta, 47 of 47 did (`sec6_parquet.log`, `sec6_delta.log`).
- Governance. HDFS authentication is `simple`; MongoDB runs with access control disabled, bound to 127.0.0.1; the Delta log records what and when but no user; HDFS trash is off (`sec10_acl.log`, `sec10_reveal.log`, `sec10_gov.log`). 19 of the 258 zones in `zone_summary` have fewer than 10 trips and 6 have exactly 1 (`sec10_reveal.log`).

## Options considered
1. Everything in Spark over Parquet/Delta on HDFS, including the web page lookups. Rejected for Q1.
2. Everything in MongoDB, including the trips for Q2. Rejected for Q2 and for reproducibility.
3. Split (chosen): MongoDB for the Q1 serving copy; Delta Lake on HDFS for the trips, the zone summary and the corrections, as the source of truth.

## Decision
- Zone figures behind the web page: MongoDB collection `bda.zone_profile`, one document per zone shaped for the page (name, borough, totals, 24 hourly fares), index on `zone_id`. It is a derived copy, rebuilt from the Delta tables by a batch job (`m1_load.py`), never edited directly.
- Trips for Q2 reports: Delta table(s) on HDFS (Parquet files plus `_delta_log`). Reports record the table version they read and are reproduced with `versionAsOf`. Retention is set so last month stays readable: `delta.logRetentionDuration` and `delta.deletedFileRetentionDuration` of at least 35 days, and VACUUM only with a retention of at least that (a setting decided here, not measured).
- Late corrections from the stream: applied to the Delta table `lake/zone_summary` with one MERGE per micro-batch; the stream keeps its own checkpoint, separate from the table's log. After each MERGE the MongoDB serving copy is refreshed from Delta.
- Access: `lake/*` tables are `chmod 750` with read-only ACL entries for named readers, never write.
- Minimum cell size: a zone with fewer than 10 trips is not served with its own figures; the page shows "fewer than 10 trips" instead of an average.

## Evidence
- Q1 latency: MongoDB (indexed) median 0.4 ms, p95 0.7 ms over 1,000 lookups; Spark on Parquet 131.3 / 157.6 ms and Spark on Delta 315.3 / 518.8 ms over 20 (Section 9.2, `sec9_compare.log`).
- Q1 under load: MongoDB p95 0.472 ms at 1 thread (3,412.3 req/s) and 2.646 ms at 4 threads (2,540.8 req/s), 11.219 ms at 16 threads; Spark on Parquet p95 64.745 ms (1 thread) and 47.916 ms (4 threads), never under 10 ms (Exercise 9, `ex09_loadtest.log`, `out/loadtest.csv`).
- An index matters once the collection is large: on 2,724,143 trip documents, zone 132 on 15 January fell from 2,724,143 documents examined and 632 ms to 5,379 and 5 ms with `{PULocationID: 1, pickup_date: 1}` (Exercise 7, `ex07_index.log`); on 258 documents it changed only the documents examined (258 to 1), not the time (median 0.491 vs 0.425 ms, Section 8.3, `sec8_lookup.log`).
- Q2: Spark on Parquet best 0.19 s against MongoDB aggregation 1.04 s over the same 2,724,143 trips, answers agreeing to within 2.0e-13 dollars (Section 9.2, `sec9_compare.log`).
- Columns: summing one column read 3.3 MB of Parquet against 356.5 MB of CSV (Section 4.2, `sec4_plan.log`); February repeats it, 3.5 MB against 356.0 MB (Exercise 1, `ex01_measure.log`).
- Reproducibility: the same path read at version 0 gives 258 rows and 404,725 trips for zone 132, and at the latest version 260 rows and 404,850 (Section 5.3, `sec5_history.log`).
- Readers during writes: overwrite of plain Parquet 0 of 149 whole reads, Delta 47 of 47 (Section 6); append of plain Parquet 56 of 57 whole and 27 of 33 with 64 files per append, Delta 15 of 15 and 29 of 29 (Exercise 8, `ex08_*.log`).
- Corrections by MERGE: 5 corrections updated 3 rows, inserted 2 and copied 255 (Section 5.2, `sec5_merge.log`); 7 corrections updated 4, inserted 3 and copied 254 (Exercise 6, `ex06_merge.log`). The table rejected an append with an extra column and a VACUUM with no retention (Section 5.4, `sec5_history.log`).
- Access refusal I produced: user `2374802010097` was refused `ls` on `lake/zone_hour` under `chmod 750` (`Permission denied ... access=READ_EXECUTE`), allowed after `setfacl -m user:2374802010097:r-x`, and still refused deleting `_delta_log` (`access=WRITE`) (Exercise 4, `ex04_acl.log`; same on `lake/zone_summary` for `reader`, `sec10_acl.log`).
- Small cells: 19 of the 258 zones in `bda.zone_summary` have fewer than 10 trips and 6 have exactly 1; zone 44's "average" is one trip of 264.1 dollars and 57.2 miles (Section 10.3, `sec10_reveal.log`).

## Consequences
- Two copies of the zone figures (Delta and MongoDB) must be kept in step; a correction reaches the page only after the refresh job runs, so the page can be stale between a MERGE and the refresh (the delay was not measured).
- MongoDB adds a second system to run, back up and secure; it stores the trips at 3.8 times the Parquet size on disk (`sec9_trips.log`), which is why it does not hold the trips.
- Every MERGE rewrites whole data files: a small correction to a large file copies the whole file (255 of 258 and 254 of 258 rows copied above). Large tables need files small enough, or partitioned, so corrections stay cheap.
- Delta keeps old files until VACUUM, so disk use grows with the retention chosen.
- Withholding small cells removes 19 zones' figures from the page.

## Limits
1. One laptop, one request stream at a time except in Exercise 9, warm caches, data that fits in memory; the load generator was a single Python process on the same machine as the servers, so the Exercise 9 rates are limited by the client as much as by MongoDB.
2. One month of trips (2,724,143 rows) for Q2; growth to years of data was not measured. Q2 was measured on Parquet (`curated`), not on a Delta copy of the trips, so the Delta overhead on Q2 is not measured (on Q1, Delta was slower than Parquet: 315.3 vs 131.3 ms median).
3. No write load during the Q1 and Q2 timings; what a page sees while the refresh job rewrites `zone_profile` was not measured.
4. The streaming path was not run on this laptop: the Lab 3 checkpoint `stream/ck/restart` does not exist here (`sec7_logs.log`), so "MERGE per micro-batch" is designed from the batch MERGE runs, not measured as a stream.
5. Security is not established: HDFS believes any user name (`simple`), so the ACL stops mistakes, not attackers; MongoDB has no access control; the Delta history has no user (`userName` NULL, no user key in `commitInfo`). The retention setting and the 10-trip threshold are decisions, not measurements.

## Revisit when
- Q1's 95th percentile on MongoDB, measured with `src/e9_loadtest.py` at 4 threads, passes 10 ms (it was 2.646 ms on 2026-11-19), or
- one MERGE of a correction batch copies more than 1,000,000 rows (`numTargetRowsCopied` in the table history; 255 today).

## What the final project carries forward
The final project keeps the split measured here: Delta Lake on HDFS as the single source of truth for trips, the zone summary and its corrections, and MongoDB only as a serving copy shaped for the page. It keeps `lake/zone_summary` and the collections `zone_summary` and `zone_profile`, and the scripts in `src/` as the evidence. Three things are still open and will be measured there rather than assumed: Q2 on a Delta copy of the trips over more than one month, the end-to-end delay from a streamed correction to the page, and how a page reads `zone_profile` while it is being refreshed. The access rule and the minimum cell size of 10 carry over unchanged, and the limits above (simple authentication, no access control in MongoDB, no user in the Delta history) are stated in the project's architecture section rather than hidden.
